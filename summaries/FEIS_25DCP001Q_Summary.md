## Analytical framework

The EIS evaluates a **Reasonable Worst-Case Development Scenario (RWCDS)** —
the maximum development that could result from the Proposed Actions — across
technical areas (land use, community facilities, open space, socioeconomic
conditions, hazardous materials, water/sewer, transportation, air quality,
noise, construction, public health). Impacts are classified as **no
significant adverse impact** or **significant adverse impact**; mitigation is
then identified, and some impacts remain even after mitigation.

## Key findings (industrial-land relevant)

### Hazardous Materials (Ch. 10)
- Former and current industrial activity creates potential soil, groundwater,
  and vapor contamination on selected development sites.
- **E-848 (E-Designation):** before any change of use or redevelopment
  requiring ground disturbance, the owner must conduct a **Phase I
  Environmental Site Assessment and subsurface testing, and remediate where
  appropriate, to the satisfaction of the Mayor's Office of Environmental
  Remediation (OER)**. DOB permits cannot be issued without OER approval.
- With those controls, significant adverse hazardous-material impacts are
  expected to be avoided.
- **Teaching point:** an old or low-FAR industrial lot is not automatically
  redevelopment-ready.

### Land Use, Zoning, and Public Policy (Ch. 2)
- The rezoning's land-use framework, including the M1→M1A conversions and the
  preservation of nonresidential zoning in the IBZ's industrial subareas
  (see `OneLIC_Summary.md`).

### Transportation (Ch. 14)
- **Significant adverse impacts** identified involving traffic, transit, and
  pedestrians.
- Industrial implications: added competition for curb space, loading areas,
  intersections, bridge approaches, and streets already carrying truck
  traffic. Mitigation is described — but don't assume every impact is fully
  mitigated without reading the mitigation tables.

### Noise (Ch. 17)
- **Significant adverse mobile-source (traffic) noise impacts** and temporary
  construction-noise impacts.
- **E-848 imposes building-envelope attenuation** and related requirements on
  selected sites.
- Some noise impacts remain unavoidable even with mitigation.
- **Teaching point:** introducing sensitive uses near truck routes and
  industrial operations requires receptor-side controls (quieter buildings)
  — it does not itself remove the underlying industrial noise source.

### Mitigation (Ch. 21)
- Consolidates the mitigation commitments across technical areas. Read this
  chapter before claiming any impact is "fully mitigated."

## The three-way distinction (the segment's core skill)

1. **Impacts** — what the Proposed Actions would cause (Chapters 10/14/17).
2. **Mitigation** — what would reduce them (Chapter 21 + each chapter's
   mitigation section).
3. **E-Designations (E-848)** — enforceable conditions attached to specific
   sites: hazmat investigation/remediation + building-envelope attenuation.
