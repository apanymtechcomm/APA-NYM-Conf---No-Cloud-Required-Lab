# Summary — OneLIC: Long Island City Neighborhood Plan (the rezoning)

**Status:** Adopted by the City Council **November 12, 2025**; ULURP complete.
**CEQR:** 25DCP001Q. **ZAP project:** 2024Q0304.
**Source:** OneLIC Final Plan Book (`OneLIC_Final_Plan_Book.pdf`, ~69 MB — full
document available in the doc pack if you want to check any citation below).

## What OneLIC is

An area-wide rezoning of the Long Island City / Court Square area, covering
blocks adjacent to — and in some cases overlapping — the Long Island City
Industrial Business Zone (IBZ). It pairs new housing and commercial capacity
with the retention of industrial zoning in the IBZ's designated industrial
subareas.

## What changed

- **Special Long Island City Mixed-Use District** — modified and expanded.
- **Manufacturing-to-mixed conversions** — portions of older M1 districts were
  rezoned to new **M1A** paired manufacturing/residential districts
  (text mapping: M1-3/4/5/6 → M1-2A/3A/4A/5A/6A).
- **Mandatory Inclusionary Housing (MIH)** area established.
- **Housing:** approximately **14,700 dwelling units**, including roughly
  **4,300 income-restricted** (plan text carries the precise figures: 14,699
  units; 3,245–4,867 affordable).
- **Commercial/community-facility/light-industrial space:** approximately
  **3.8 million sf** (plan text: 3.72M sf).
- **The IBZ's industrial subareas remain nonresidential.** OneLIC does not
  eliminate the IBZ — it adds mixed-use capacity at its edges while the
  designated industrial core keeps prohibiting residential development.

## What it means for the IBZ

OneLIC creates mixed-use and housing capacity *near* parts of the IBZ while
preserving nonresidential zoning *inside* the designated industrial subareas.
The combination can increase land-value and compatibility pressure at the IBZ
edges even where the formal boundary and zoning hold. Parcel-level
consequences (contamination review, loading/truck conflicts, noise
receptors) are documented in the companion FEIS — see
`FEIS_25DCP001Q_Summary.md`.
