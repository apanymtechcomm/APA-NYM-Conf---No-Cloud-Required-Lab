# Summary — NYC Industrial Plan (citywide industrial policy)

**Final release:** December 19, 2025. **Required by:** Local Law 172 of 2023.
**Source:** `NYC_Industrial_Plan_Final.pdf` (~63 MB — full document available in
the doc pack).

## What it is

The citywide industrial policy plan — the policy counterpart to the
neighborhood-level OneLIC rezoning. Where OneLIC governs one area's zoning,
the Industrial Plan sets citywide strategy for retaining and modernizing the
industrial job base.

## Structure

- **5 goals, 20 strategies, and 72 "ideas for improving the industrial
  economy"** (the plan's own wording).
- **Source discrepancy worth flagging:** the city's project page describes
  "73 recommendations" while the plan text says 72. When sources disagree,
  the document wins — and cite which one you read.
- Goal themes include: enabling industrial businesses to **evolve, innovate,
  and modernize** (reducing red tape, investing in capacity building,
  workforce development programs), plus freight, public-realm, and
  climate-resilience work.

## Land-use framework — read this carefully

The plan distinguishes:

- **Primary Industrial Areas** — areas with strong freight connectivity and
  industrial concentration
- **Secondary Industrial Areas**
- **Neighborhood industrial areas**

**These are policy guideposts, not zoning districts.** They signal where the
city intends industrial land to remain, but they are not mapped through a
land-use action and do not change what's permitted on any parcel. Only a
rezoning (like OneLIC) changes zoning. Industrial Business Zones (IBZs),
including the Long Island City IBZ, are part of the city's designated
industrial land: **over 17,000 acres** citywide, protected under this
framework.

## What it means for the LIC IBZ

- The citywide policy direction is industrial **retention and modernization**
  — consistent with the IBZ's industrial subareas staying nonresidential
  under OneLIC.
- But policy intent is not regulation: compatibility pressure at the IBZ's
  edges comes from the neighborhood rezoning, not from the citywide plan.
- The plan's freight/public-realm/climate themes are the levers an IBZ
  stakeholder would engage at the citywide level.
